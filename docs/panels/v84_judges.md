**Recommended combination.** Two cheap, independent tests in the first days, then one carried object measured with P5's zero-jumpstart rule on a repaired fresh climber.

All of this fits on CPU at about 3 GB RAM. A positive would show faster learning of what matters, carried as questions rather than answers, which is the most console-portable reading of the ladder.

### P5 Answers stay home (zero-mean carried covariance + same-experience exam) — score 7.5

- Strengths: Best fit to the owner's human-like property: what is carried is the hypothesis space (which ego-centric, action-rotated features tend to matter for points and deaths), not the answers. It is colour-free and ego-centric, so it is the most likely carried object to survive a console change, provided the same body and view pipeline exists on NES. The zero-mean prior gives a jumpstart of exactly zero, with a prefix-identity certificate; it is the only design whose construction rules out the head-start confusion. The controls separate content from generic anisotropic shrinkage and from tuned defaults: P (shifted labels, spectrum-matched), U (input PCA, spectrum-matched), and a prior-scale scalar tuned symmetrically for F as well. The K* ceiling is inside the gate. Presentable: 'from the same experience, the experienced agent reaches the novice's end-of-session understanding sooner', plus readable eigen-notions. It reuses the most existing assets: G0 costs zero emulator frames on the 82 A1 logs, uses the v83 views, the 427 features and the promise head code, uses the record lineages for the exam, and keeps v83's L2-SP prior as the X-mean contrast. I verified its data claims: the A1 npz files do hold row_*, iter_*, rec_actions and base_state (room_job_v83.py:78-82); alien 9131 has 22,638 rows; A1 runs were unsteered (steer null), so the streams do not depend on any arm. G0 can fail: it needs at least 12 units, K* R >= 2 and X >= 1.5 against the per-unit best of F, P and U.
- Flaws: A positive shows faster learning of short-horizon consequence prediction or one-step slot choice from a frozen stream. It does not show faster play or climb; the live leg is secondary and given about 3%, so the ladder moves only on 'understanding', not competence. The mechanism is classic multi-task covariance or feature learning (Argyriou, Evgeniou & Pontil 2008, not cited; hierarchical Bayesian priors). Only the measurement is new. The A1 logs are not virgin: craft_v6_out/v83/explore/promise_store and run.log show an exploratory cross-game GOOD/BAD pass already ran on them ('good alien False'). G0's thresholds must be frozen before anyone reads that result, otherwise it breaks the rule against setting criteria after observation. v80 found that predictions did not beat climatology robustly, and many A1 streams are near-degenerate (krull 3.6k rows; gopher 9131 15 cells; hero 14 cells; wizard_of_wor 9131 11 cells). The 10% odds of PARTIAL or better look optimistic.
- Graft: Zero-mean (sign-symmetric) carried object as the house rule for any carried prior, with a prefix-identity certificate. Same-experience comparison as the noise-free first measurement. U and P spectrum-matched controls. The prior scale tuned symmetrically for every arm, as the tuned-defaults guard. The counterfactual savestate exam with evaluator-held truth. Levels anchored on F's own end competence.

### P2 Carried skills (savestate-verified egocentric macros) — score 7

- Strengths: Strong ladder logic: dodge, approach, sidestep-and-fire in canonical slot space with rotation and reflection invariance is exactly the motor know-how humans carry from Atari to NES and 16-bit. It is the most presentable carried object (a named, printable skill library, the winning families, a dose curve). G0 is cheap (about 92M frames), can fail, and is decisive. It controls the 'more options' effect with 50 matched random libraries and cross-fitting on anchor halves, and it has a deep-tercile clause against early-only gains. Its diagnosis of A1 checks out against the bars: centipede constant 9,318.7 against V's best of 3,652 and 5,403; on atlantis, krull, kung_fu_master, phoenix, pooyan, time_pilot and wizard_of_wor the bar comes from uniform play; krull ran only 208 and 159 rollouts; qbert took 773 s. It reuses the adapter, the views' foreground window, V as the anchor generator, and the climb stats.
- Flaws: G0 measures a level (24-step yield at anchors), not a rate. 'Better tools' at an anchor can still give only a head start in the climb, and the argument that the fresh learner cannot invent the option is asserted, not tested until G2. Mining needs per-step action strings that A1 does not log (rows only every 4 steps, rec_actions only for record lineages), so it relies on new anchor runs. There is no tuned-defaults comparator beyond the designer's fresh menu: a library that just rediscovers 'tap fire on shooters' is designer-level knowledge. P2 itself predicts most of D comes from AWAY/PERP+TAP on shooters, which presents as a game-genre default. Novelty is moderate (Konidaris & Barto portable options, Rosman & Ramamoorthy action priors, temporally-extended epsilon-greedy as the placebo). The closed-loop rules depend on 'me' and probes, which A1 showed cost 27% of frames.
- Graft: A CRN savestate-branch portability test against innate modes and matched random libraries, before any climb. An early versus deep anchor split. The skill library as a candidate carried object expressed as zero-mean or flat-prior options, so it adds no jumpstart. A dose curve over the number of source games.

### P1 Search sense (DAC controller over a portfolio explorer) — score 6

- Strengths: It has the best controls for separating transfer from tuned defaults: T (a clock-only tuned schedule), S (the single best operator), a norm-matched placebo, and an explicit TUNED-DEFAULTS verdict. The fresh-learner fixes map one-to-one onto A1 lessons: bar modes as operators, continuing after a life loss, K_time and K_scr progress axes, a probe token bucket. Its factual base is verified: the best bar mode across the 41 games is constant 17, uniform 16, held 6, zaxxon zero, with jamesbond a tie it does not list; alien 1,386,720 probe frames for 1,080 probes; probe share 26.7% of 164M; name_this_game 9132 has 2 cells. G1 can fail, and its se_proj ≤ 0.21 bound is tight given A1's σ² of 1.165. G1b is a zero-frame filter. Everything runs on CPU at about 3 GB. It makes a falsifiable evidence-scarcity prediction (the advantage grows along the ladder). The carried object is pixel-free and so console-agnostic.
- Flaws: The carried knowledge is about configuring a savestate search, not about games or play, so a positive is algorithm configuration (DAC transplant, admitted) and hard to present as 'learns new games like a human'. It is also weakly tied to the pixels-to-play vision. The designer predicts the most likely failure: the in-run bandit converges in a few hundred rollouts and leaves only a head start. Overall odds of about 2-3% admit it. G1b's one-step IPS value inside a sequential search is a weak proxy. The eight hand-designed operators make F much better, but that is designer knowledge, not learning. A1 logs are barely reused.
- Graft: The T (clock-only tuned schedule) and S (single best operator) controls, and the TUNED-DEFAULTS verdict. Bars that use only processes the explorer plays. K_time as a progress axis that depends on neither me nor score. The probe token bucket. Rungs set at fractions of F's own height, so none exceeds a game's maximum. The F-against-F instrument line.

### P3 Climb first, then carry the search sense (V2 Go-Explore + expansion critic) — score 5

- Strengths: The fresh learner is the most robust: no probes and no 'me'; frame-code cells give a progress axis that would work on any console; play continues after a life loss; and the ladder is built on cumulative true rises, which fixes pong, fishing_derby, ice_hockey and tennis. G0 is very cheap (48M frames, about 1.5 h) and can fail. The A3′ WORTH gate (K* ceiling) asks whether search selection is the bottleneck at all before any cross-game claim, and the dose arm checks accumulation. Its numbers check out: entry 0.082-0.194M frames, peak commit 0.86-1.25 GB, median wall time about 650 s. It is honest that the expected verdict is HEAD START ONLY.
- Flaws: V2 is Go-Explore's exploration phase and the critic resembles learned branch-and-bound node selection and Intelligent Go-Explore. The proposer admits it did not check the literature, so novelty is lowest after P4. Dropping 'me', views and promise heads discards v83's distinctive perception assets and moves away from ego-centric knowledge that could carry across consoles. The G0 panel is 10 failure games chosen after seeing A1, with a 4M cap and a revision allowed. That is fine as exploratory work, but it is tuning on known failures. There is no clock-only or tuned-defaults control: the carried slopes can encode generic heuristics such as 'prefer high S-rank'. The likely deliverable is a feasibility result (a Go-Explore reproduction), which presents poorly as 'faster learning'.
- Graft: Frame-code cells with adaptive resolution as the fallback cell, independent of 'me'. Continuing after a life loss under the same lives rule as the bar. Cumulative true rises as the ladder score. The WORTH (K*) gate placed before any cross-game arm. Rung-averaged climb counted from each arm's own M1.

### P4 Carried eye, fresh hand (GPU value learner, encoder-only carry) — score 4

- Strengths: It is the only design that yields a live pixel player, the explicit ARC 4 vision, and so steps around the distillation wall. Live learning curves are the most presentable format. Resetting the head with a matched-level yardstick (T(L3/4)−T(L1/4)) and a same-game K* ceiling with capture ratio φ are sound. The proposal is honest that the literature predicts weak or negative transfer (plasticity loss) and predicts KC-dev itself.
- Flaws: It adopts a published pipeline (Atari-100k-class DER/SPR, multi-game pretraining as in Actor-Mimic, MGDT and Scaled QL), against the owner's 'innovate, do not adopt pipelines'. What it carries (pixel encoder features, an 18-action Atari joystick) is the knowledge least likely to survive a change of console. It reuses almost nothing of v83 (explorer, views, promise heads, A1 logs), only the senses ledger and the climb stats. P_shuffle is a near-trivial placebo: pretrained weights against norm-matched noise mostly measure generic visual initialisation. The more telling P_scramble arm only feeds a caption. Compute risk is the highest: the GPU is shared with Tavern, host commit is about 2.8-3.5 GB (top of the 2-4 GB budget), and building a vmapped SPR learner plus pilot in 3-4 days is optimistic.
- Graft: The capture ratio φ = log R(X:F)/log R(K*:F) as a reported diagnostic. The outcome-scramble attribution arm, kept as a real control rather than a caption. A live-play secondary readout for presentability.

### Proposal 5: Answers stay home (zero-mean carried covariance prior, same-experience exam) — score 8

- Strengths: Best first gate of the five. G0 runs offline on the 82 existing A1 logs: zero emulator frames, about 3 h of CPU, a verdict in 1-2 days. I checked the inputs it needs and they are real. The A1 npz files hold row_view, row_slot, row_it, row_rise_lag/row_drop_lag, iter_billed, base_state and rec_actions. alien_9131 has 22,638 rows, and the 82 runs hold about 5.2M rows in total, which matches the claim of about 5M. The gate can clearly fail: it needs at least 12 units, K* at 2.0x or more, and X at 1.5x or more against the per-unit BEST of F, P and U with a lower bound above 1.1, plus a late-climb lower bound above 1. The controls are the strongest of any proposal for separating transfer from tuned defaults: a tuned isotropic F, a shifted-label spectrum-matched placebo P, and an input-PCA spectrum-matched U that captures generic anisotropic shrinkage. The zero-mean prior makes the jumpstart exactly zero, with a prefix-identity certificate, so the yardstick cannot mistake a head start for faster learning. Every arm learns from the same stream, which removes the search chaos behind lesson 6 (pooled sigma 1.08). That makes this the only design with a realistic chance of adequate power. Compute is very comfortable (about 0.3G billed plus 0.45G evaluator frames, 2 CPU workers, no GPU), and the sealed test is mostly offline.
- Flaws: 1) The claim is narrower than the owner's goal: it shows faster learning of short-horizon consequence prediction and exam choice from the same experience, not faster learning to play. The live leg it offers (PLAYS-SOONER) has about 3% odds and is a reactive policy driven by the heads, close to the wall v72-v74 hit. 2) Its odds arithmetic is overstated. Its own chain (0.7 x 0.45 x 0.6 x 0.65 x 0.75 x 0.7) gives about 0.065 for PARTIAL, not the stated 0.10, and about 0.046 for 2x. 3) It leans on a weak signal with a poor record: v80 predictions did not beat climatology, v83 itself predicted that the prior's value fades with rows, and the pending exploratory cross-game run (explore/run.log) has so far logged only 'good alien False'. 4) The exam rebuilds evaluator states by replaying lineages. That depends on the note-21 NOOP-before-restore fix (5 of 6 Q*bert replays had diverged), and it excludes states where 'me' is lost, which drops many states on atlantis, krull, up_n_down and qbert (lost_frac 0.56-1.0). 5) The 19 log-spaced checkpoints are coarse (a factor of about 1.41 per step), so a 1.5x ratio is about one checkpoint, and quantisation noise matters.
- Graft: Use G0 as the day-1 zero-frame filter for ANY carried content. Use the same-experience paired design as the instrument for offline predictive gates. Use the input-PCA spectrum-matched control U and the per-unit best-of-controls comparison. Treat a zero-mean or sign-symmetric carry as the way to rule out a head start by construction.

### Proposal 3: Climb first, then carry the search sense (V2 Go-Explore with frame-code cells, cross-game expansion critic) — score 7

Compute is about 2.1G frames, about 65 h on 3 workers at 0.8-1 GB each, which fits.

### Proposal 4: Carried eye, fresh hand (GPU DQN/SPR live learner, encoder-only carry with head reset) — score 5

- Strengths: G1 is a real and valuable test: a live pixel learner must reach S >= 2B, as a live mean rather than a savestate best, against bars matched to its own process. For ARC 4's vision this is the most useful by-product of any proposal. Resetting the head makes the reading a slope measure. The C between L_1/4 and L_3/4 is a proper matched-level climb. G2 WORTH (a same-game encoder must give at least 2x) is decisive before any cross-game claim, and the P_shuffle and P_scramble controls are sensible. Host RAM (about 2.8 GB) and VRAM (about 4.7 GB) look plausible. The designer predicts R(X:F) of about 1.1-1.3, which is honest.
- Flaws: 1) It carries the highest engineering risk: DQN with SPR, vmapped over 6 runs, a GPU uint8 replay buffer and shared-memory ALE workers on Windows, built in 3 days or less, on a GPU shared with Tavern. A silent bug degrades learning without failing a test. 2) The throughput arithmetic is optimistic. 16k frames/s means about 4,000 synchronous inference round-trips/s, plus about 167 vmapped SPR updates/s each taking several ms, all in one learner process. 6-8k frames/s is more realistic, which puts the 1.5G-frame plan at about 55-70 h of emulation-bound time and leaves G3 and G4 at risk inside 14 days. G1 and G2 still fit. 3) The designer's own predictions put G3 below its threshold (it needs 1.5x with a lower bound above 1.1). The literature it cites (Actor-Mimic, Progressive Nets, plasticity loss) also points to small or negative cross-game encoder transfer, so the odds of a positive are about 2% or less in two weeks. 4) The bars are max-of-modes (centipede 9.3k, video_pinball 24.5k, atlantis 14.7k), which makes S >= 2B hard for a 500k-step DQN on several games. The noisy 5-episode trailing mean on long episodes (up to 28k frames each) makes first-passage times coarse. 5) This is an adopted pipeline, which sits poorly with the owner's wish for innovation.
- Graft: Keep the live TD learner as a v85 or ladder platform once a carried object has passed offline. Use the head-reset slope reading, the WORTH same-game ceiling and the capture ratio phi. Use P_scramble as an attribution arm for outcome content.

### P1 Search sense (learned controller over a portfolio explorer) — score 8

- Strengths: Has the strongest controls against the tuned-defaults confound. T is a clock-only tuned schedule learned on the same dev games, S is the single best operator, the comparator is the better of T and S per game, and the verdict ladder has an explicit TUNED-DEFAULTS label. The placebo is norm-matched, keeps the clock, confidence and switching, and breaks the content. Controls are LOGO in dev, and a dose response (5/10/20/40 games) is reported at G1b. Rungs R1..R4 sit between B' and H, where H is the min of two F eligibility seeds. Because F set them itself, no rung can be out of reach (fishing_derby, pong and freeway are fixed by construction). The climb counts from each arm's own R1, the late climb from R2, and HS is reported but never claimed. A falsifiable profile prediction is written down: X's advantage grows with j while T looks like a head start. Sealed: 8 seeds each for X, F and T, an F-vs-F instrument line and a censoring INCONCLUSIVE rule. Every verdict is a conjunction (intersection-union), so multiplicity does not inflate. It states plainly that a true 1.5x effect passes PARTIAL only about half the time. All six A1 lessons are answered. The bars max over 4 modes the explorer itself plays (O1/O2/O3/O5). 'Continue' operators archive after a life loss. Probes are capped at 8% by a token bucket. K_time and K_scr add progress axes. CPU only, about 1.5 GB per worker, which fits. Factual claims I checked hold: alien probes 1,386,720 frames for 1,080 probes; krull probe share 0.88/0.90; name_this_game 9132 has 2 cells; video_pinball uniform 24,454 vs repeat 431; road_runner constant 980 vs uniform 10.
- Flaws: G1b is a kill gate built on one-step self-normalised IPS estimates inside a sequential search, where an operator choice changes the future archive. The estimate is biased in an unknown direction, so it can wrongly kill a real late-climb effect or wrongly pass one. It is declared a filter, but it still decides KC-select. The placebo permutes behaviour features only within each game. The game-constant adapter-signature features (movement slots, spawn slot) stay intact, so P keeps between-game content and is partly real. That makes X:P conservative, but it muddles the GENERIC reading. The G1 eligibility is much looser than A1's: H >= B'+2u' (about 2B) instead of M3 (about 5B). Height room is swapped for a frame-duration clause (d). It is frozen before data, but 18/35 here is not comparable with A1's 6/35. When H is barely above B'+2u', the rungs between B' and H are thin and T(R) is very noisy. The mechanism (operator choice) is a priori mostly head start, because the in-run bandit converges within a few hundred rollouts. The proposal admits this and gives about 2.5% odds. Minor: the bar-mode tally is off by one (my count from the bar files is 18 constant including zero ties, 17 uniform including the jamesbond tie, 6 repeat).
- Graft: Make the clock-only tuned schedule (T) and the single-best (S) controls mandatory in any design. Anchor rungs on the fresh learner's own height. Write the per-level ratio profile as a prediction. Keep the TUNED-DEFAULTS and HEAD-START labels in the verdict ladder. Keep the se_proj power gate calibrated to the number of seeds. Keep the F-vs-F instrument line with 8 seeds.

### P5 Answers stay home (zero-mean prior covariance, same-experience exam) — score 7

- Strengths: Has the best internal validity for separating faster learning from a head start. All arms learn from the same frozen stream, so search chaos (lesson 6) is removed. The prior is zero-mean, so the jumpstart is identically zero, with a prefix-identity certificate. The placebos keep structure and break content well. P uses shifted labels with Sigma's spectrum, and U is input PCA with Sigma's spectrum. The comparator is the per-unit best of F, P and U. One prior-scale scalar is tuned per arm, which removes v83's confound of an over-regularised F. I checked this: v83 F used n0 = 50,000 L2 toward 0, which slows F by construction. K* is a same-game ceiling and gates WORTH. Levels are fractions of F's own end competence, and a ceiling guard is added. Time-to-level must hold for two checkpoints. The exam has a test-retest reliability instrument (< 0.8 gives INCONCLUSIVE). Ground truth stays evaluator-side. G0 costs zero emulator frames, and the data claims check out: the A1 npz files hold row_view, row_slot, row_it, rise/drop lags, iter_billed, base_state and rec_actions, and alien 9131 has 22,638 rows. Compute is tiny.
- Flaws: Construct validity is the big gap. The measure is the sample efficiency of a 24/40-step GOOD/BAD predictor plus a one-step slot-choice exam, not learning to play a new game. The brief's lesson 1 (a fresh learner that climbs) is sidestepped by redefining the learner. The live leg is secondary, at about 3% odds. Leakage in the G0 WORTH clause: K*'s Sigma comes from full-stream heads fitted on the other stream, then AUC is scored on 5,000 rows of that same stream. That inflates K* and makes the K* R >= 2.0 room clause easier to pass. G0 also runs on A1 logs that the v83 exploratory cross-game GOOD/BAD analysis (docs/ARC4_V83_EXPLORATORY.md, run in progress) is mining right now. The data is burned and the analysis path can fork unless G0 is frozen before that output is read. Units are (game, head) pairs. Two heads per game are correlated, so the bootstrap must cluster by game, and that is not explicit. Choosing X-cov or X-rep at G0 adds multiplicity at the filter. There is no dose arm. Exam states come from one best-record lineage, so they are highly correlated. E can be dominated by a constant best slot: the share is only reported and not controlled in the exam levels. The predicted gain fades as n grows past d (same as v83's A2b), so R_late is fragile. The 10% odds look optimistic. Many A1 streams are short (krull 3.6-4.7k rows, road_runner about 9k), which limits units.
- Graft: Add a zero-frame, same-experience 'knowledge clock' as an early mechanism filter. Use spectrum-matched shifted-label and input-PCA placebos for any carried statistical prior. Tune regularisation per arm so the fresh arm is not handicapped. Use a zero-mean or prefix-identity certificate wherever a carrier can be built to start identical to F. Require a 'held for two checkpoints' rule on every first-passage time. Use exam-style test-retest reliability as an instrument check.

### P4 Carried eye, fresh hand (GPU value learner, encoder-only carry) — score 6

- Strengths: The only design whose yardstick is live play from reset, the closest construct to 'learns a new game'. The Q-head is reset and the epsilon schedule is the same in all arms, which limits head start. Levels are anchored on F's end score: L1/4 to L3/4 for the climb, L1/2 to L3/4 for the late climb, HS at L1/8, reported only. The K* WORTH gate requires R(K*:F) >= 2, lower bound >= 1.25, before any cross-game arm. A P_scramble attribution arm (outcomes shifted >= 5,000 steps) separates outcome knowledge from visual and dynamics knowledge. 2-fold LOGO in dev, an instrument line and a futility stop. The A1 failure modes are solved naturally: no probes or cells, every life played as in the bars, bars built from F's own action modes. Predictions are written honestly (phi about 0.2, likely KC-dev).
- Flaws: The only placebo a verdict requires is P_shuffle, which permutes entries within each tensor. Trained-weight scale used as a random init can be pathological for plasticity, so P_shuffle is likely a straw man and X:P_shuffle adds little beyond X:F. There is no dose arm and no generic warm-start control (pretraining on one game, or on spatially scrambled frames). The design therefore cannot tell game-general content apart from generic pretraining conditioning, and P_scramble is caption-only. First passage of a 5-episode trailing mean has no hold rule, so it is noise-biased. Deep-RL seed variance at 2M frames is large. With the arm cap at 2M and L3/4 set from F's last 10%, many runs will be censored, which risks INCONCLUSIVE. Compute fits only on paper: the GPU is shared with Tavern, and a vmap DQN+SPR+shared-memory pipeline is to be built in 4 days. P0 tunes replay ratio and lab fraction on the calibration games. That is shared across arms, but it is another tuned default. The pipeline is adopted, with little innovation.
- Graft: Make live-play curves anchored on F's end score a secondary yardstick that ties any search-based claim back to game play. Keep the K* same-game ceiling and report the capture ratio phi = log R(X:F) / log R(K*:F). Keep the outcome-scrambled pretraining twin as an attribution arm.
